The 11 companies TRC named, reviewed live in the browser on 23 October 2026. Two rounds: site structure and audience split first, then each homepage section by section. Toptal was added as a global benchmark for two-sided talent sites.

## Findings that shaped the plan

**A jobs board is not the norm.** 8 of 11 do not run one. HuntingCube is a product company, not an agency. Careernet keeps its board on a different domain and brand. Talent Toppers' jobs page mostly lists recruiter vacancies at Talent Toppers itself, plus an embedded Economic Times RSS feed.

**ABC validates the candidate-database model.** Its candidate page says it maintains an active database of leaders and matches them to opportunities. No roles are listed, only a registration form. Its form has 16 visible fields, takes Current CTC as a single number, and also asks Gender and Date of Birth. TRC should not copy the last two: neither helps a match and both create avoidable discrimination risk.

**The audience split has a proven pattern.** ABC's navigation has a "Get In Touch" menu with three doors: I Am Hiring, I Am Seeking Leadership Roles, I Have Other Queries. HuntingCube uses two top-level items, Job Seekers and Employers. Keeping the split in the navigation on every page is stronger than a home-page-only pair of buttons.

**Service-page depth is where the search competition is.** Success Pact runs 13 service pages, including Technology, Engineering, Product, Data, Infra/Ops and UI/UX Designer Hiring as separate URLs. Zyoin runs 10. Purple Quarter has one page per job title: CTO, CPO/CTPO, CISO, SVP/VP Engineering, Head of Engineering, Director of Engineering, Country/Site Head, Engineering Manager, Chief Architect, and an AI and data leadership cluster.

**The quality bar is low in places.** Archelons is four pages, with font-loader test strings ("BESbswy") visible in the page. GetHyr is a single page where every nav link points to itself. Pylon is a Joomla site with a 2004 to 2009 copyright line. A clean, fast site is ahead of these three on day one.

## Homepage patterns

| Pattern | How many | Who |
|---|---|---|
| Numbers strip | 8 of 11 | ABC, Careernet, Purple Quarter, Anzy, HuntingCube, Zyoin, Success Pact, GetHyr |
| Testimonials | 7 of 11 | ABC, Careernet, Anzy, Zyoin, Success Pact, HuntingCube, GetHyr |
| Loads a slider library | 7 of 11 | ABC, Careernet, Purple Quarter, Anzy, HuntingCube, Zyoin, Success Pact |
| Video on the homepage | 4 of 11 | Purple Quarter, HuntingCube, Zyoin, Success Pact |
| FAQ | 3 of 11 | Anzy, HuntingCube, Zyoin |
| Enquiry form on the homepage | 2 of 11 | Anzy, Zyoin |
| Two audiences split on the homepage itself | 2 of 11 | Zyoin (hero buttons plus a popup), HuntingCube |
| Live chat widget | 0 of 11 | |

**Headings are weak across the market.** Of the 10 live homepages checked for headings (Pylon excluded), only 3 have one H1 that says what the firm does: Purple Quarter, HuntingCube and Success Pact. ABC, Careernet and Archelons have no H1. Anzy has 2 and GetHyr has 5. Zyoin uses its H1 for the client-logo heading, and Talent Toppers uses the company name.

**Section order seen most often:** hero, numbers, services, process, testimonials, client logos, final call to action, footer.

**Footers:** Purple Quarter lists offices in India, USA, UAE and Singapore, plus resources and a newsletter. Success Pact lists two full office addresses. Toptal uses its footer as a link hub to every "hire X" page.

**Toptal (benchmark):** a single-audience hero ("Hire Top Talent"), with the talent-side path kept secondary in the header and footer. Sections run hero, value, process in three steps, talent profiles, services, testimonials, publications.
